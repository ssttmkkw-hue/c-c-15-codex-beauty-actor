## Round 2 critique

I reran the production capture and opened the Round 1 panel, two-shot, both lead portraits, expression tests, a spoken frame, the navy dress, the rain setup and the mobile layout.

- The two-shot looks like two character cards against a stock sunset. His gaze plays to camera while she looks into the gap. Their shoulders end on ruler-straight vertical cuts, especially obvious in the rain setup and the navy dress. The giant pink sky, scattered confetti and matching scale flatten the space between the two bodies.
- The resting portraits are the prettiest frames. As soon as a character talks, the lips turn into a dark chip pasted over a soft, airbrushed patch. Surprise makes the same chip into a diamond. The close-up exaggerates the difference between the finely illustrated eyes and the generic vector mouths. Sadness has neutral brows and two symmetrical white teardrop stickers. Smile and shyness barely change the eyes, and her blush is almost fluorescent.
- The close-ups are center punches with the same radius of background blur. Eyes and mouth share a glare-heavy pink wash with the backdrop. The rain version just dims the sunlit portraits against blue scenery. The old peach rim still reads as sunset, and none of the signs appears to light the cast.
- The subtitle gradient muddies the wardrobe in the lower third. Two opaque corner labels and a viewfinder overlay make the stage read like a screenshot from an editor. On mobile the control grid squeezes wardrobe and camera names into a narrow column, and the English subtitle competes with the Chinese line for the little space available.

## Round 3 critique

I rebuilt Round 2, reran the complete capture as `r2-review-`, and opened the panel, mobile layout, two-shot, both resting close-ups, anger, surprise, sadness, wardrobe, spoken frames, rain and the ticket reveal. The rerun measured 59.6 FPS on the rooftop and 56.2 FPS in the rain.

- The close-ups sell the cast. Then the camera puts two catalogue cutouts side by side, with their hair almost the same size and both chests open to the audience. Pupils alone cannot sell that they are facing each other. Frame after frame redraws the same shoulder line, and the reveal is a line of text over another version of his portrait. The scene has nowhere to look when the ticket enters the story, and it never gives the two leads a private moment after the last line.
- The resting mouths have painted planes and color. The moving mouths look dusty and flat at the corners, sometimes with a skin-colored oval visible below the nose. Male anger has a pinched brow and a gray slash for a mouth. Her surprise changes her whole eye shape in one step. The sad close-up works as a still, but the identical almost front-facing camera on every reply keeps it feeling like a pose selector.
- The blurred solo backgrounds are a wall of purple, with the same glaring orange sun behind either face. The rain grade makes both characters lavender from forehead to neck. Petals sometimes land on eyes or mouths as though the picture were dirty. The near and far particles need visibly different focus and less activity near the eyes.
- The panel promises a short film, but the scene starts with a whole sentence printed over a frozen exchange and shows the punch line before it is spoken. The desktop controls are crowded against the picture. On a phone, the names and both subtitle lines eat into the portraits just when the wardrobe and their shoulders should establish the setting.
